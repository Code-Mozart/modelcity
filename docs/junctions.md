# Junctions (^157): the rules, and the testing ground

**This file is the one place for the rules of junctions.** Rented hats: the rules ^1003 to ^1015, the cases of the testing ground ^985 to ^1001 and ^1020 to ^1021. Status `decided` = the user's words, `assumed` = Claude filled it in.

## Principles and scope

| Hat | What | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ^1003 | **Junctions are derived, by rules (user).** Everything a junction is and shows (its area, the roles of its lanes, its markings, stop lines, arrows, islands) is derived from the stored model (nodes with headings, segments with a direction, a radius and a lane configuration) by the rules of this file. Nothing about a junction is stored; the data model does not change. Where a rule cannot decide, or two readings are possible, it is listed under *Ambiguities* and goes to the user. | decided |
| ^1004 | **Scope of the milestone (user).** In: the islands and holes of the surface and what happens to them for now (to be customised later); what a junction is; the **lane markings inside junctions**; stop lines, give-way lines and arrows; whatever it takes to detect and classify (splits, merges, turns). Out: fundamental or structural performance work (^927); validations (^827); road styles (^964), though the rules must hold for every kind of car lane (trams, bike paths and the like are out for now). If the milestone grows past about 2000 added lines it is cut into parts. First step: the classification of ^1006 to ^1009, shown only in the debug layer (^1010). | decided |
| ^1005 | **What a junction is (user; more to come).** A junction is where two or more segments intersect. Several intersections whose segments start or end in the same tower count as one junction. (Claude, to be made precise with the user: whether a split or a merge alone, where segments leave one node without crossing, is a junction; whether segments that cross without a node, ^994 and ^995, are one.) | decided (the sentence); open (the details) |

## Classification (the first step)

All of it is judged where the points are (^853, the one position tolerance ^865, the heading test `alongOrAgainst`), with no new number.

| Hat | Rule | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ^1006 | **The manoeuvre of a segment.** What a segment does from its start node to its end node, as it is travelled. Seen from the start node (its position and heading): the end node heads **another way** (not the same, not the opposite): a **left turn** or a **right turn**, by the sum of the turning of its arcs; the **opposite** way: a **u-turn**; the **same** way and on the line of the start heading: **straight**; the same way but beside that line: a **shift left** or a **shift right** (a lane change, an S-curve). A manoeuvre belongs to the segment, not to a node: it is the same at both its ends. | assumed |
| ^1007 | **Pulling through: the main.** At a node, an incoming and an outgoing segment (of the same lane configuration) *pull through* when the curve does not change its bend at the node: straight goes on straight, or an arc goes on along the same circle. Those two are the **main** at that node. If a segment has more than one such partner (a lane that goes straight on and one that goes straight a little and then turns), the partner that keeps to the common curve longest is the main. This is narrower than a *node without an effect* (^891), which also allows a straight followed by a bend. | assumed; **not built now** (built and removed again in PR #53: nothing uses it since the signs, ^1012; it returns when the main lane matters, with ^1014) |
| ^1008 | **Splits, merges and equals.** A node with three or more segments is a **split** (more segments out than in), a **merge** (more in than out) or **both**. Its segments that are not main split off the main, or merge into it: this is which lane merges into which. If no pair pulls through, the segments split or merge **as equals** (the end of the side road of a T: a left and a right turn; a fork). | assumed; **not built now** (as ^1007) |
| ^1009 | **Lane extension and narrowing.** At a split, an outgoing *shift* whose end node lies beside another outgoing segment of the node (it touches it, ^818: one lane width away, heading along it) is a **lane extension**: a lane is added beside it. At a merge, an incoming *shift* whose start node lies beside another incoming segment is a **narrowing**: a lane ends. A shift that ends beside nothing is just a shift. | assumed |
| ^1010 | ~~Words in the debug layer under the nodes of splits and merges and on their segments.~~ Removed (user): the signs say it (^1012, ^1015). | withdrawn |

## The road layout and its signs (the user's design, ^1012 to ^1015)

The **road layout** (user's term) is what the segments are for the traffic on them, deduced from the graph by the **road layout rules** below; a **sign** shows the road layout of a segment. Every segment has a sign: what it is for the traffic on it, after the German signs of the prescribed directions (Zeichen 209, 214) and the boards for lanes that are added or end (Zeichen 541, 531). "Straight" here means *goes on*, not *has no bend*: the segment may be a bend, an S-curve or a hook.

| Hat | Rule | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ^1012 | **The signs (user).** Read at the **end node** of the segment (the last node of its branch, ^1013). **Straight** (209-30, the default): nothing else applies, e.g. it is the only segment of its end node. **Straight and right** (214) / **straight and left** (214-10): the end node leads into a straight segment and a right (left) turn. **Straight, right and left** (209-30 with 214-30): into a straight segment, a right and a left turn. **Split** (214-30): the end node splits into a left and a right turn, or into two segments that both go on. **Right** (209) / **left** (209-10): the end node is a merge and this is its right (left) leg. **Extension left / right** (after 541-10 / 541-20): the end node splits into two segments that are parallel, even if only for a part, and the left (right) one is an S-curve: a lane is added there. **Merge into right / left** (after 531-20 / 531-10): this segment is an S-curve that merges with the segment to its right (left), to which it is parallel, even if only for a part; the end node is a merge and this is its left (right) leg. | decided |
| ^1013 | **How it is decided (answer to the user's question: yes, it is decidable).** (1) *Left and right leg of a merge:* a right turn always comes from the right of the heading it ends with, a left turn from the left (for any turn of less than half a circle), so **the leg and the turn are one fact**: the right leg is the segment whose manoeuvre (^1006) is a right turn, the left leg a left turn; a u-turn by the way it turns. A leg that shifts sideways comes from the side it moves away from; if it begins beside another leg (^1009) it is the lane that ends and *merges into* the side it moves to. The leg that goes straight on has no leg sign. This needs no look at where a branch ends, so it also works for a turn that does not end in a merge. (2) *What follows a split:* each outgoing segment by its own manoeuvre: a turn is left or right, anything else goes on. Two outgoing segments of which one shifts and ends beside the other: an extension on the side of the shift. Only segments that go on, or only turns (also two turns to the same side, ^1020: one of them is still further left than the other): a split. Else straight with the sides that are there (also for more than three segments). (3) *Order (user, ^1024):* if the end node is a split, the sign of the split, **also when it is a merge as well**: a turn is a turn (left or right) when it ends in a node that only merges; if that node also splits, what follows matters more, and every leg shows the split. Else a leg that is not straight on shows its leg sign; else straight. *The quarter-turn rule (user agreed, ^1023):* of two turns to opposite sides, one of **less than a quarter turn counts as going on if the other is more than a quarter turn**: straight and left, or straight and right (the arm of the fork ^993 that meets a gentle left and a sharp right). Two gentle turns (the stem of a fork), two quarter turns (the side road of a T) or a quarter turn against a gentle one (^1021) stay a split. A quarter turn is judged by the headings of the nodes (^865), no new number. (4) *Segment or branch, a mix:* the sign is read at the **last node of the branch** (^128: through nodes with one segment in and one out), so every segment of a branch shows the same sign; the leg is judged by the **last segment** of the branch and what follows by the **first segment** of each branch that leaves, because a long branch may turn many times far from the junction. | assumed |
| ^1014 | **Which branch is longer (user, for later).** When it has to be decided again which lane is the main one (^1007, the ambiguities of ^1011), an important measure is which branch is longer or has more segments. Not used yet. | decided (as a measure to use) |
| ^1015 | **Drawn in the debug layer (`Ctrl+Period`).** One sign at the middle of every segment: a disc 40 px across with white arrows, in the debug red (^926; the German signs are blue, the debug layer has one colour, ^805), the same size at every zoom. All signs are round, and upright on the screen: up on the sign is up on the screen, whichever way the segment or the map is turned (user). The sign of the hovered segment is named under the coordinates (^1017, in `docs/hats.md`). | decided (round, upright, size); assumed (the drawing) |

**Per branch or per segment?** The user's guess was per branch. The rules of ^1006 to ^1009 are per **segment at a node** (the signs are read per branch, ^1013): a branch (a chain of segments without splits or merges, ^128) can be long and turn many times, and a segment between two junction nodes is the first of its branch at one end and the last at the other; what matters at a junction is how a segment leaves or reaches the node. The manoeuvre (^1006) is one per segment; main, extension and narrowing (^1007, ^1009) are per segment and node. In the usual case, a branch of one segment, it is the same thing.

## Ambiguities (^1011, for the user)

| Hat | What the rules cannot decide, or decide in a way that may surprise | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ^1011 | (1) **Which lane is the added one is a matter of how it is drawn.** In the turn pockets of the ground (^987, ^990) the pocket goes straight on and the through lane shifts a lane to the right: the rules call the pocket `main` and the through lane an `extension`. A person would say the pocket is the added lane. Geometry alone cannot tell; if the main must be the lane that carries the through traffic, it has to be drawn straight (the pocket shifting left), or a later rule must look further (which lane goes straight on *across* the junction). (2) **Two lanes that both go straight on** from one node for the same length before they part: no longest partner; today the first found is the main (the length of the branch, ^1014, could decide). (3) **A gentle turn against a sharp one**: with nothing pulling through they are equals, however different. (4) **A main road that bends where a side road goes straight on** (a motorway exit on a curve): the rule follows the circle, so the curving road is the main, which is right; but a side road that leaves on the same circle and the main road that straightens would be told wrongly. (7) and (8) are decided and built: the quarter-turn rule and the node that is a merge and a split (^1013, ^1023 and ^1024 in `docs/hats.md`). (9) **Roundabouts** (^996, ^1001) are not detected: their segments are plain `straight and right` and `right`. An idea for later (^1022 in `docs/hats.md`). (10) **Five arms** (^999): left as it is, to be looked at with the markings of part 2 (user). (5) **A turn drawn as several segments** whose last segment is straight reaches its merge as the straight leg and shows no leg sign (^1013 reads the last segment). (6) **A turn pocket** shows *extension right* on the lane before it, because the through lane is the one that shifts (see 1). | open |

## The testing ground (^984)

One project with the common kinds of junction, for the milestone of the junctions (^157): `docs/mocks/hat984.json` (import it in the projects modal). It is **made by code**, `src/lib/testing/junctionGround.ts` with the kit `junctionKit.ts` (arms with lanes, joined lane by lane), so a case can be added or changed in one line; `junctionGround.test` keeps the file equal to the code (`UPDATE_MOCKS=1 bunx vitest run src/lib/testing/junctionGround.test.ts` writes it again) and checks that it opens and that every segment has a curve. Nineteen cases.

Right-hand traffic. Every lane in of an arm is joined to the lanes out of the other arms: a left turn from the innermost lane to the innermost, a right turn from the outermost to the outermost, straight on lane by lane. All radii are automatic. The cells of the grid are 260 m apart to the right and 240 m down; the center of a cell is the center of its junction.

## What it shows today (before the milestone)

Seen by drawing the surface and the markings of the ground (v0.52.0):

- **Markings run through junctions.** Lanes that go straight on across a junction are parallel there too, so their line crosses the junction (^988 to ^990); turning lanes that are concentric (left and right turn of a T, ^985) get a curved line between them inside the junction.
- **Holes in the middle.** Where the hulls of the turning lanes do not cover all of the junction, small holes of land stay in the surface (^990: four in the middle).
- **Gaps that are islands.** The gap a turn pocket leaves (^987, ^990) and the middle of the roundabout (^996) are holes in the surface: this is where traffic islands (^27) would be.
- **Violations.** The validator counts 26 violations in the ground: in a junction a node is often one lane width from a turning curve that heads another way (^818). The rule is right between junctions and wrong inside them.
- **Crossings without a node** (^994, ^995) are one surface with all markings drawn across each other; they belong to the milestone of the crossings (^158).
- **Speed.** The surface of the whole ground takes about 0.2 s and its markings 0.07 s on every change of the graph (the performance milestone, ^927).
